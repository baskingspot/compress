# Agent notes: snappy

`README.md` is the user-facing contract; this file holds the reasons behind it.

## Design decisions

- The stream framing is this package's own, not Google's `sNaPpY` framing with
  CRC-32C chunks. A concatenation of raw blocks is not self-delimiting, so each
  block gets a `u32-le` length prefix.
- No magic, end marker, or checksum, on purpose. The remedy for an undetected
  cut or a concatenated stream is a checksum container around the stream (as
  gzip wraps flate), not more framing here.
- The `Reader` and `Writer` entries are generated by `internal.reader.VTable`
  and `internal.writer.VTable`; the `std.Io` contract traps behind them are in
  `src/internal/AGENTS.md` ("Io contracts"). Two matter most here: the writer's
  `rebase` must emit what it frees, never discard it (a direct-slice write onto
  a full buffer lands there), and `Reader` returns `StreamTooLong`, never an
  assert, on an oversized consumer request.
- `Reader` rejects a framed block that declares more than one block of output
  (`InvalidStream`): the room its slide guarantees is one block, so a hostile
  stream could otherwise push `dest` past the serving region. Acceptance rules
  are commented at `fillNextBlock`.
- S2 (klauspost's `s2/`) stays out. It decodes Snappy, but its own stream is not
  Snappy: repeat offsets (a copy-1 tag with offset 0 reuses the previous offset;
  here it is `DecompressionFailed`, as in Snappy) and blocks up to 4 MiB. The
  encoder ports only the Snappy-compatible `encodeBlockSnappyGo64K`, which emits
  explicit offsets (its `repeat` variable is a match-finder shortcut). S2 is a
  deferred decision (`docs/slop/plan.md`, non-goals), not a missing
  feature.

## Format notes

- Spec §2.2 (`docs/specs/snappy-format-description.txt`): the reference
  compressor never emits offsets over about 32768, and a decompressor must not
  rely on that. The decoder handles copy-4 and large offsets anyway. This
  encoder's offsets fit `u16` (blocks are at most 64 KiB), so `emitCopy`'s
  copy-4 branch is unreachable from `compressBlock`.

## Testing and golden vectors

- The decode vectors are ported from golang/snappy (`golden.zig`) and reused by
  every layer: the block decoder, the framed `Reader`, and `Writer` output
  re-decoded block by block.
- `TestDecodeCopy4` (copy-4 at offset 65540) is the only golden case with a real
  large offset. The `TestDecodeLengthOffset` sweep drives `copyMatch` through
  every small offset and length, overlap included.
- Each golden decode case pre-fills the target with `internal.sentinel` and
  checks that no byte past the decoded length changed, pass or fail.
- Encoder output is never compared byte for byte with another encoder: the
  format has no canonical encoding (golang/snappy's tests say so; note in
  `decode.zig`). The encoder is tested by round trips through our decoder.
- `Writer.zig` and `Reader.zig` each run a seeded random driver over the `Io`
  vtable contract; `fuzzStreamMachinery` is the fuzzer version of the writer's.

## Oracles

- There is no `just snappy-oracle` (flate, gzip, and zlib have `just *-oracle`).
  The golang/snappy decode vectors are the interop bar.
- The bench corpus's `.snappy` blobs come from this encoder and are decoded by
  the klauspost and google/snappy arms (`bench/README.md`, "Corpus"). `just
  bench-test` runs each arm's `--check` on the EC2 boxes.

## Performance notes

- Local `just bench` numbers are not claims; claims cite runs under
  `docs/slop/results/`. Locally, decompress is slowest on RLE (many short overlapping
  copies, one shuffle each) and fastest on incompressible input (plain literal
  copies).
- To check codegen, run `zig build bench`, find the runner
  (`find .zig-cache -name bench-snappy -type f`), and
  `objdump -d` it on AArch64. Look for `tbl` (the overlapping-copy
  shuffle; GNU objdump prints `tbl v0.16b, ...`, so do not grep `tbl.16b`),
  `ldr q` / `str q` (16-byte moves on the literal and non-overlapping copy
  paths), and `eor` + `rbit` + `clz` (`@ctz` in the 8-byte match extension).

## Provenance

`THIRD_PARTY.md` records the ports. The decoder is original Zig; google/snappy
(`snappy.cc`, `snappy-internal.h`, `snappy.h`) was studied, not copied, for the
SIMD overlapping-copy technique (`pattern_masks` and `reshuffle_masks` are the
Zig equivalents of `pattern_generation_masks` and `pattern_reshuffle_masks`) and
for the block and hash-table constants.
