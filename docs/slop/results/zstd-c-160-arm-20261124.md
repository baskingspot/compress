# The zstd-c arm at v1.6.0: a local A/B on Neoverse V3, 2026-11-24

v1.6.0 (2026-11-23) carries AArch64 decoder work on the Neoverse cores
(`#4413`, `#4414`, `#4418`, `#4509`), and the fleet's zstd-c arm was
still v1.5.7. This measures what moving the arm costs the zc ÷ zstd-c
decode ratios before a fleet run. The numbers are local and are not
claims.

Box: the dev machine, Neoverse-V3 (MIDR `0x410fd841`), 8 cores, no
isolation. Both libraries built from their release tarballs with the
fleet's cmake configuration (`build/cmake`, static, no programs, tests,
or threads, `CMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release`) through a wrapper running
`zig cc -mcpu=native`. The same `bench/drivers/c/bench_zstd.c` compiled
against each library with `zig cc -O2`; zc built with `zig build
fleet-bench -Dtarget=aarch64-linux-gnu -Dcpu=neoverse_v3
-Doptimize=ReleaseFast`. Inputs are the committed `bench/corpus-gen`
tree, generated by the pinned 1.5.7 CLI and verified against
`SHA256SUMS`. Three interleaved runs per binary, 12 samples per cell at
150 ms, `--filter zstd/decompress`; the table is the median of the 36
samples per cell. Per-cell coefficient of variation was 0.0–0.6%.

| case | zc MB/s | 1.5.7 MB/s | 1.6.0 MB/s | zc÷1.5.7 | zc÷1.6.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| html-32K | 12740 | 7048 | 7047 | 1.81 | 1.81 |
| html-64K | 12936 | 7056 | 7019 | 1.83 | 1.84 |
| mixed-32K | 13961 | 12738 | 12566 | 1.10 | 1.11 |
| mixed-64K | 14071 | 11991 | 11985 | 1.17 | 1.17 |
| prose-32K | 892 | 1432 | 1208 | 0.62 | 0.74 |
| prose-64K | 1003 | 1592 | 1319 | 0.63 | 0.76 |
| random-32K | 13455 | 12732 | 12581 | 1.06 | 1.07 |
| random-64K | 13925 | 11992 | 11982 | 1.16 | 1.16 |
| rle-32K | 13311 | 3057 | 3800 | 4.35 | 3.50 |
| rle-64K | 13642 | 3054 | 3792 | 4.47 | 3.60 |
| skewlit-32K | 887 | 1408 | 1473 | 0.63 | 0.60 |

The difference is not a uniform speedup. v1.6.0 decodes RLE 24% faster,
words 23% slower, and prose 17% slower; skewlit gains 5%, and the rest
sit within 1.5%. On the ratios, the RLE win narrows from 4.4× to 3.5×,
the words gap narrows from 1.55× to 1.20×, and skewlit widens slightly
from 1.59× to 1.66×. The geometric mean of the ratio change is 1.024:
the C's position improves 2.4% on average while individual rows move
20%.

The local zc and C binaries link glibc; the fleet builds musl. c8g
(Neoverse V2) and the Intel targets are unmeasured, and no fleet run
happened: `just bench-run --up` fails here because
`~/.ssh/baskingspot-bench.pem` is missing.

## The oracle CLI at v1.6.0

A locally built 1.6.0 CLI run through `just zstd-oracle` stops at the
DT1 dictionary-boundary row. The 1.5.7 CLI decodes all three hand-built
boundary frames; 1.6.0 refuses each with "Data corruption detected". The
cause is the external-history bound in `#5023`: once a full window has
been decoded, no dictionary history remains. The RFC `§5` wording this
decoder follows is "less than or equal to Window_Size", so ours accepts
the frame whose match starts exactly at the boundary. When the CLI pin
moves to 1.6.x, that row needs a new expected verdict, and the boundary
reading becomes a decision: follow the C's exclusive bound or keep the
RFC's inclusive one.

## Corpus regeneration

`just corpus` currently fails before generating: the flake's brotli CLI
is 1.2.1 and `bench/zig/corpus.zig` pins 1.2.0. The existing
`bench/corpus-gen` verifies against `SHA256SUMS`, so the committed corpus
stays usable. Regenerating it under the new CLIs changes the manifest and
the cross-run comparison, which is a decision, not a repair.

## A C pattern that does not transfer

v1.6.0's AArch64 `ZSTD_decodeSequence` keeps the three repeat offsets in
registers across the sequence loop and writes them back at the end. The
same move in `sequences.zig` — a local `[3]u32` across `executeSequences`,
passed by pointer to `resolveOffset`, with the state write-back at the
loop's end (tried with a `defer` and with an explicit store after the
loop) — measured slower on V3:

| case | 32K | 64K |
|---|---|---|
| words | 0.87 | 0.88 |
| prose | 0.93 | 0.91 |
| rle | 0.98 | 0.96 |
| html/text/mixed/random | 0.98–1.00 | 0.98–1.00 |

Median of 36 samples per cell, three interleaved runs against the
unpatched `zc` build, the same corpus and 150 ms samples; the two
variants agreed within noise. Reverted. The loss is on the shapes with
the most sequences per block, which reads as register pressure in a loop
that already holds three FSE states, three cells, and the bit reader;
the state array is hot and the store is cheaper than the spill.
