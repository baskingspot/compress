## The verdict: the copy-structure shapes won, the entropy shapes lost

- **The copy-structure shapes are won**: rle 0.11-0.51 everywhere, text/html on every ARM
  target (0.45-0.94) and at parity on Intel at 64K (0.64-0.69). The C decoder always
  decodes into its ring and copies the output out; our one-shot decodes in place — the
  mechanism the census predicted, and these are microsecond rows (13-52 byte streams for
  text/html/rle), so they are also the most code-layout-sensitive.
- **`words` (the only dictionary+transforms row) is 2.3-2.8x behind on every target** —
  121-161 static-dictionary references per stream ride the transform path. The census
  named this row "solid" as an exercise of the dictionary; the decode of it is the
  entropy+dictionary path and it is the ranked gap.
- **`skewlit` (literal Huffman) is 2.9-4.0x behind** — the peek-table decode per symbol
  against the C's two-level table, the same per-symbol loop edge the zstd decode side
  knows.
- **`mixed/random` are 6-13x behind, and the mechanism is an open question.** These
  streams are one uncompressed meta-block plus headers (mixed-65536.br is 65,541 bytes
  of a 65,536-byte raw file) — the row should be a bulk `fastmem.copy` (verified: the
  takeBytes path is a bulk copy, not byte-at-a-time) plus the ladder, and the census's
  two-copies-against-one predicted we would win. The ratio shape says otherwise: our
  time is near-constant across 32K and 64K (32K rows at 11-13x, 64K at 6-8x — half the
  input, double the ratio), which means a per-call cost of roughly the 32K row's whole
  time dominates, independent of input size. The candidates are the decode path's
  per-call fixed costs (the ~707 KB State's stack-frame handling among them) and the C
  arm's near-memcpy path. The brotli decode perf wave owns the diagnosis; no claim is
  made here beyond the measured grid.

The 14-row geomean is quoted only as "this corpus's mix" — never a decoder-speed
headline: ten of the fourteen rows are mostly copy structure, per the census.

Missing competitors: klauspost/compress ships no brotli and std ships none; the M6 run
races google/brotli's C decoder only, with andybalholm/brotli v1.2.6 as the oracle
lane's Go reference by decision, not an arm.

Follow-ups recorded for the brotli decode perf wave: the mixed/random mechanism (ranked
first — an unidentified per-call cost), the words dictionary path (2.3-2.8x), the
skewlit per-symbol decode (2.9-4.0x), and a natural-text corpus shape (append-only)
before any general "brotli decode is faster" claim — no current shape exercises block
switching or context modeling at scale.
